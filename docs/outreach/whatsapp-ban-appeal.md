## Be clear-eyed about the odds

Write the appeal — it costs an hour and the account is worth having back. But plan as
though it will not be restored.

Bans issued under the "recent account activity doesn't follow our Terms of Service" wording,
where **"Requesting a review is not available"**, are the hard category. That line means the
self-service path is closed. Meta's enforcement here is largely automated, appeals are read
at volume, and reversals are uncommon. Sympathetic circumstances do not usually change the
outcome, because the system is not evaluating intent.

So: send the appeal, and **in parallel** rebuild distribution on channels that cannot be
switched off. The second is the one that actually protects the campaign.

## Who sends it — Gaurang, not Bhavin

**Every letter here must go from Gaurang, from the email address registered to the banned
account.** This is not a formality:

- Meta acts only on requests from the **account holder**. A third party writing about
  someone else's account is closed at triage, or answered with "the account holder must
  contact us" — costing days.
- Sending **from the registered email** is itself a weak ownership signal, and one of the
  few the reviewer has.
- Under the IT Rules, the complainant must be the **person aggrieved**. A grievance filed by
  a non-party has no standing, and the GAC appeal that depends on it inherits the defect.
- Both letters are written in the first person as Shaurya's father. That voice is doing real
  work, and it cannot be borrowed.

**What Bhavin can do:** draft the letters (done), sit with Gaurang while he sends them,
be **cc'd** so replies are visible and the log stays current, and prepare the GAC filing —
though the GAC submission itself must also be made by Gaurang.

If Gaurang would rather not compose in English, Bhavin can type and send **from Gaurang's
own email account, with his permission**. That is still the account holder's address, which
is what matters.

**Do not send parallel letters from several people.** Multiple senders on one case get
merged and deprioritised, and can read as brigading. Keep it single-threaded from Gaurang.

## Before writing — one thing to check

The screen says **"Date requested: 8 Aug 2026"**, but Gaurang associates the lockout with
**15 August**. Ask him whether he already submitted a review request, when, and what came
back. An appeal that presents itself as a first request when a review has already been
declined will be dismissed on sight.

## The escalation ladder — and the part most people never use

Because Gaurang is in India, he has a **statutory** route that ordinary support email does
not give him. Work down this ladder in order; each rung is a precondition for the next.

| # | Route | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ~~`support@whatsapp.com`~~ | **DEAD — attempted 18 Aug 2026, [bounced](../sources/2026-08-18-whatsapp-support-bounce.md).** The address does not accept support requests. Every alternative it suggests returns to the ban screen |

DLF Cyber City, Building No. 5, Tower A, Phase III, Gurgaon – 122002.*

**This ladder is the reason to be organised about dates.** The GAC will not hear an appeal
unless the Grievance Officer was contacted first, and the 30-day window runs from their
reply. Write to the Grievance Officer now, keep the acknowledgement, and diarise the date.

**One last thing worth trying in parallel — from a desktop, not the phone.** Every route
Gaurang tried opened the WhatsApp app, because mobile browsers deep-link into it. On a
laptop there is no app to open, so **https://www.whatsapp.com/contact/** may render an actual
web form. It takes five minutes, and either outcome helps: a working form is another channel,
and a failure is one more documented attempt to attach to the grievance.

Do not wait on it. **Send the grievance now** and try the desktop form alongside.

## Do not do these

- **Do not tap "Register new number" on Gaurang's existing SIM** hoping to regain the old
  account. It will not restore anything.
- **Do not set up a new WhatsApp account to carry on the same broadcasting.** Evading a ban
  is itself a violation, and if the new account is linked to the same device or activity it
  will be banned faster — this time with a pattern of evasion attached to the family's name.
  For a campaign whose entire asset is public trust, that is a genuinely bad trade.
- **Do not use "bulk WhatsApp sender" tools or unofficial clients.** They are the single
  most reliable way to get banned permanently.

## Why the letter is written this way

- **It admits the conduct.** Appeals that deny obvious bulk messaging get closed. Accepting
  responsibility and showing understanding of *why* the rule exists is the only posture that
  ever moves a reviewer.
- **It is verifiable.** Two live campaign links, a named hospital, a named consultant. A
  reviewer can confirm the story in under a minute, and that is what separates it from the
  thousands of unverifiable hardship claims Meta receives daily.
- **It does not beg or threaten.** No legal language, no publicity threats, no emotional
  escalation beyond the facts. The facts are already extraordinary.
- **It asks for one specific thing** and offers evidence rather than attaching it unasked.

The **grievance letter** does the same job in a different register. It is formal because it
has to function as a record: numbered paragraphs, the statutory basis stated, a specific
relief sought, and an explicit request for a ticket number. If the matter later goes to the
GAC, that letter and its acknowledgement are the evidence the appeal rests on. WhatsApp's
grievance process also asks that complaints carry an **electronic signature** — include one.

## Regardless of the outcome — fix the dependency

The campaign lost its main distribution channel overnight because that channel belonged to
someone else. Rebuild so that cannot happen again:

- **Never mass-forward from a personal number again.** Not Gaurang's new number, not
  Jolly's, not yours.
- **Let supporters do the forwarding.** A message shared by fifty people to their own
  contacts reaches further than one person messaging fifty strangers, and it is exactly the
  behaviour WhatsApp is built to reward rather than punish.
- **Use WhatsApp Status and Channels** — designed for one-to-many, and safe.
- **Move the canonical link off WhatsApp.** A single page with the combined total, both
  campaign links and the documents is the thing to share. That is the platform, and it is
  the reason to build it.
- **Check the flyers and QR posters.** If they carry the banned number, they now point
  nowhere. Reprint with a working contact or a link to the page.

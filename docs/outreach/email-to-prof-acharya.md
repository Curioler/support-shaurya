**Subject:** Seeking your opinion — DMD (exon 50 deletion) in a 4-year-old, son of a close friend

---

Dear Sir,

I hope this finds you well. This is Bhavin — I was your student in biochemistry at
`[institution, batch year]`. I have thought of you often over the years, usually when
something turns out to hinge on the fundamentals you taught us. This is one of those times,
and I am writing to ask for your view.

The four-year-old son of a close friend, Gaurang Nayak, has been diagnosed with Duchenne
muscular dystrophy. His name is Shaurya. I have been helping the family organise the
medical information and the fundraising effort, and I have reached the limit of what I can
reason about on my own.

**The molecular picture:**

- MLPA (MedGenome, Bangalore, 19 May 2026): **hemizygous deletion of exon 50** of *DMD*,
  dosage quotient 0.00, reference transcript NM_004006.2 (Dp427m). Classified pathogenic.
  No VUS, and no other copy-number change anywhere across the gene.
- **Out-of-frame** by the reading-frame rule, consistent with a Duchenne rather than Becker
  phenotype.
- CPK **22,051 U/L** (age reference 75–230), measured 1 May 2026.
- Clinically: ambulant, early phase. Vignos 2, Brooke 1. Gowers' sign positive,
  hyperlordosis, calf hypertrophy. Independent walking achieved by age 2. Weight 14.27 kg.
- Deflazacort 12 mg daily (≈0.8 mg/kg/day) was due to begin in early July.

**Where the family is:**

His treating neurologist at SPARSH Bengaluru has prescribed **ELEVIDYS** (delandistrogene
moxeparvovec) — 14 vials, a single infusion — at a cost of about **₹26 crore, excluding
taxes and import duties**. Two crowdfunding campaigns are running and have together raised
a little over ₹11 lakh. The arithmetic is what it is.

Meanwhile, because the deletion is exon 50, he appears to sit in the best-served mutation
class in the whole disease — skipping exon 51 restores the reading frame. From the trial
registry, there is a Phase 3 exon-51 antisense study recruiting, a gene-editing study that
takes deletions within exons 45–55 in his exact age band, and a micro-dystrophin gene
therapy trial open to any DMD mutation. None is recruiting in India.

**The four things I would value your opinion on:**

1. **Is my reading of the frame logic sound** — that a single out-of-frame exon 50 deletion
   is precisely the case exon-51 skipping was designed for, and that this genuinely widens
   his options rather than being a technicality?

2. **Micro-dystrophin versus exon skipping.** ELEVIDYS delivers a truncated construct; the
   antisense approach restores a shortened but endogenous transcript. If you were advising
   this family, would you put the weight behind the ₹26 crore purchase, or behind getting
   him into an exon-51 trial?

3. **Anti-AAVrh74 seropositivity.** Every AAV route, ELEVIDYS included, requires the child
   to be seronegative. I have no sense of what background seroprevalence looks like in an
   Indian paediatric population — and no document in the file records that he has been
   tested. Is this as decisive a gate as I think, and is it plausible he simply fails it?

4. **The lab's own caveat.** MedGenome note that a point variation in a probe-binding site
   can mimic a single-exon deletion, and they recommend sequencing exon 50 to confirm. It
   has not been done. Would you press for it before the family commits further?

If it would help, I can send the MedGenome report, the neurology notes and the CPK report —
they are all with me.

And if you happen to know anyone working in neuromuscular genetics in India, an
introduction would be worth a great deal to this family. They have been writing to trial
sites abroad more or less blind.

I know this is a considerable thing to land in your inbox unannounced. Even a few lines
would help me think more clearly about what to advise them. Thank you for reading this far.

With respect and gratitude,

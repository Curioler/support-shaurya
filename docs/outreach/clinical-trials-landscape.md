## Two facts decide almost everything below

Most of the doors in this document are locked by two pieces of information. Until both
are known, outreach is guesswork — and both are already knowable from tests Shaurya has
likely had or can easily have.

### 1. Shaurya's exact DMD mutation

Look for the exon numbers in his genetic report — e.g. "deletion of exons 48–50".

Of the trials open to a 4-year-old, most accept **only specific mutations**:

| Requirement | Trials that need it |
|---|---|
| Amenable to **exon 44** skipping | ENTR-601-44, NS-089/NCNP-02 |
| Amenable to **exon 45** skipping | ENTR-601-45 |
| Amenable to **exon 51** skipping | DYNE-251 (FORZETTO) |
| Amenable to **exon 53** skipping | WVE-N531 (FORWARD-53) |
| Mutation contained **within exons 45–55** | PBGENE-DMD (FUNCTION-DMD) |
| **Any** DMD-causing mutation | SGT-003, Givinostat PK study |

One line from his genetic report eliminates most of this table and promotes the rest.
**This is the highest-value single piece of information in the whole campaign.**

### 2. His AAV antibody status

Every gene therapy route — SGT-003, PBGENE-DMD, and **ELEVIDYS itself** — requires the
patient to test negative for antibodies against the viral vector. Prior natural exposure
to the virus makes the therapy unusable, and the antibody test is an ordinary blood test.

The implication is blunt and needs saying plainly: **if Shaurya is AAV-antibody positive,
the ₹26 crore ELEVIDYS purchase would not be possible regardless of how much money is
raised.** Sarepta's own ENDEAVOR study and Solid's SGT-003 both screen for it. If this
test has not been done, it should be done before the fundraising target is publicised
further — not because the campaign is in doubt, but because donors are being asked for
₹26 crore toward a treatment whose feasibility rests on a test result.

If the test has already been done and is negative, that is a strong, concrete fact worth
stating publicly. It tells donors the money can actually be used.

## India

**There are no DMD gene therapy or exon-skipping trials currently recruiting in India.**

The only India-based DMD studies in the registry are stem-cell studies that are completed,
of unknown status, or withdrawn — [NCT01834066](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01834066)
and [NCT01834040](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01834040) (Chaitanya Hospital, Pune,
status *unknown*) and [NCT02241434](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02241434) (Neurogen,
*withdrawn*). Autologous stem cell therapy is not established treatment for DMD, and
families in this position are sometimes offered it privately at significant cost. Anything
along these lines should go past Shaurya's neurologists before any money moves.

Practically, this means every live trial route requires international travel, and the
campaign should account for visa, travel, accommodation and carer costs on top of any
treatment figure.

## How this was compiled

Reproducible with two calls to the public registry API:

```bash
curl "https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies?query.cond=Duchenne+Muscular+Dystrophy&filter.overallStatus=RECRUITING&pageSize=100&format=json"
```

```bash
curl "https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT06138639"
```

Studies were kept when the eligible age range included 52 months. Site-level contacts
come from each study's own `contactsLocationsModule`, which is maintained by the sponsor.

**Known limits.** Registry contact details lag reality — coordinators change jobs and
records are not always updated. "Recruiting" does not mean slots are open; gene therapy
trials of 6–32 participants often fill quietly. Eligibility criteria here are summarised,
and every trial has criteria not reproduced above. None of this replaces a conversation
with a treating neurologist.

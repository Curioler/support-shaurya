## Two things to handle carefully before this is used publicly

### 1. "Excluding taxes" — the ₹26 crore target is the floor, not the ceiling

The certificate is explicit that ₹26 crore is the drug cost **excluding taxes**, and it
says nothing about hospitalisation, the pre-infusion immunosuppression regimen, or
post-infusion monitoring. India has moved to exempt drugs for rare diseases imported for
personal use from basic customs duty, but that relief has to be applied for and does not
automatically cover every levy.

**The campaign should establish the full landed cost — drug, duties, GST, hospital,
monitoring — and raise against that number.** Reaching ₹26 crore and then discovering the
therapy cannot be released or administered for want of another sum would be the worst
possible outcome, and it is entirely foreseeable from this sentence.

### 2. The word "cure"

The certificate says "There is a cure in the form of a one-off injection ELEVIDYS", then
describes it accurately as "a gene therapy aimed at getting dystrophin protein expressed in
his muscles". Those two statements sit awkwardly together.

**ELEVIDYS is not a cure.** It is a one-time gene therapy intended to slow progression by
producing a shortened dystrophin. Notably, the family's own WhatsApp appeal already gets
this right — it says the therapy "offers hope for slowing the disease", which is accurate.

This matters because ELEVIDYS is a high-profile product that donors and journalists will
look up, and its clinical record is publicly debated. A campaign that describes it as a
cure invites a correction that undermines everything else it says; a campaign that
describes it as the best available treatment for a fatal disease is both accurate and, for
a four-year-old, entirely compelling.

**Recommendation:** use the certificate as-is where a physician's attestation is required
— platform verification, customs, institutional donors. In public-facing copy, use the
appeal's existing language rather than the word "cure". Do not alter the certificate.

## Still absent

No mention anywhere of the **anti-AAVrh74 antibody test**, which ELEVIDYS cannot be given
without. Two documents from the treating physician on the same day, and neither records it.

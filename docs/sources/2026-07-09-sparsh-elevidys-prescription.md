## Dose arithmetic — checks out

ELEVIDYS is dosed at **1.33 × 10¹⁴ vg/kg**, and the product is supplied at
**1.33 × 10¹³ vg/mL** — so the volume works out at **10 mL per kg**.

| Step | Value |
|---|---|
| Weight | 14.27 kg |
| Volume at 10 mL/kg | 142.7 mL |
| Vial size | 10 mL |
| Vials required | 14.27 → rounded to **14 whole vials** |
| Prescribed volume | **140 mL** |
| Delivered dose | 1.86 × 10¹⁵ vg = **1.30 × 10¹⁴ vg/kg** |
| Proportion of label dose | **98.1%** |

The prescription is internally consistent with the product label, rounded down to whole
vials as is standard. **14 vials** is a concrete number the campaign can cost against.

## Observations to follow up

**Weight has gone down.** 14.74 kg on 25 May → **14.27 kg on 9 July**, a loss of about
0.47 kg over six weeks in a four-year-old. Two readings on different scales are not a
trend, and this may be nothing. But corticosteroids characteristically cause weight *gain*,
so it is at least consistent with deflazacort not having started by 9 July. That is a
question, not a conclusion — and it is the same question already outstanding.

**No AAV antibody result appears anywhere.** ELEVIDYS requires screening for antibodies to
the AAVrh74 vector before dosing. A prescription being written suggests the treating team
has a plan for it, but no document in this collection records the test or its result.

**Regulatory route is unstated.** ELEVIDYS holds US approval; it is not an Indian-approved
product. Administering it at SPARSH would ordinarily require a named-patient / compassionate
import authorisation. The prescription does not say which route is being used, how long it
takes, or whether it has been applied for. For a campaign raising ₹26 crore, that pathway
matters as much as the money.

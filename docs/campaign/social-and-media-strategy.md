## The strategic shift

Prakriti's conclusion, reached independently of the numbers:

> "If it reaches American and European audiences the need will be met sooner."

The arithmetic agrees. ₹11.5 lakh in about three weeks from Indian small donations puts the
campaign at roughly ₹2 crore in a year. **₹26 crore does not come from that curve.** It comes
from international reach, a few very large gifts, or both.

So the working model changes shape:

| Channel | Job |
|---|---|
| ImpactGuru + Ketto | Indian donors, tax benefits, verification credibility |
| **Chuffed** | **International and diaspora donors** — Prakriti calls it the most transparent and internationally trusted |
| Instagram / social | The engine that drives all three |
| **The Better India / The Logical Indian** | The step change — national reach, and the credibility that unlocks CSR and government |

## Sequencing — do not skip this

**Fix the campaign pages before pitching any journalist.** Right now a reporter checking the
story finds two different goal figures (₹20 crore vs ₹26 crore), Shaurya's name used for his
mother on Ketto, and an inverted relationship on ImpactGuru. Editors at The Better India and
The Logical Indian verify before publishing. Contradictions do not produce a cautious
article — they produce no article, and you rarely get a second pitch.

Order:

1. Fix both campaign pages *(a few days)*
2. Publish one page with the combined total *(see [hosting.md](../platform/hosting.md))*
3. Open Chuffed for international donors
4. Build the social presence
5. **Then** pitch the media

## Instagram and social

Prakriti's specific advice:

- Make the family's own profiles **public**, donation link in bio
- **Post consistently** — a dormant account converts nothing
- **Collaborate** with other accounts and friends; borrowed audiences beat cold reach
- Reels outperform static posts

Practical additions:

- **One link in bio** pointing at the campaign page, which then offers all donation routes.
  Not three competing links. This is the second reason to build that page.
- Post **updates, not only appeals**. "The MedGenome report confirmed exon 50" or "we've
  crossed ₹10 lakh" are shareable; a repeated ask is not.
- **Let supporters carry the reach.** A message shared by fifty people outperforms one
  account messaging fifty — and it is the behaviour platforms reward rather than punish.
  The WhatsApp ban was a lesson about exactly this.

## The question of Shaurya's photograph

Prakriti raised this herself, honestly: *"That's not the safest place to put your child's
picture, but they will get more attention."*

She is right on both halves, and it is the family's decision, not ours. What we owe them is a
clear view of the trade rather than a nudge in either direction.

**What is true:**

- Faces raise far more than text. Campaigns without a child's photograph substantially
  underperform. This is not a marginal effect.
- It is permanent. Shaurya will be an adult one day, and images posted now will still be
  findable. He cannot consent at four.
- **Photographs from medical fundraisers are routinely stolen for fraudulent campaigns.**
  This is common enough to plan for, and it is a risk to the family's own campaign as much
  as to the child.

**If the family chooses to share images — and most in this position do — these reduce the
harm without reducing the reach:**

- Use photographs **the family selects deliberately**: Shaurya playing, laughing, being a
  four-year-old. Not hospital beds, not visible decline, not medical procedures. These also
  perform better — people give to a child with a future, not to a diagnosis.
- **Watermark every image** with the campaign URL. It makes theft harder and turns any
  reposting into an advertisement.
- Never publish the **school name, home locality, daily routine, or timetable**.
- Set a **reverse-image search reminder** every few weeks to catch stolen use early.
- Agree now what will *not* be shared later — as the disease progresses, the pressure to
  post more affecting images increases exactly when Shaurya's dignity most needs protecting.

**A middle path worth offering them:** lead with the parents' faces and voices. Jolly's
Ketto appeal is already the most affecting document in the campaign. A mother speaking to
camera carries the story with less exposure of the child, and it is her decision to make
about herself.

## The Better India / The Logical Indian

Both cover exactly this kind of story and both receive a very large volume of requests. What
gets through is a **tight, verifiable pitch**, not a long emotional one — they supply the
emotion themselves.

The pitch should contain:

- One paragraph: who Shaurya is, what he has, what it costs, what the deadline is
- **The physician's certificate** — a named consultant at a named hospital stating the
  therapy "works in a time limited fashion and works better if given earlier"
- The genetic report, showing this is documented, not claimed
- Both campaign links and the combined total
- One contact who answers quickly

**The angle that makes this a story rather than an appeal:** Shaurya's mutation is one of
the most treatable in DMD — exon 50, the mutation class the entire field was built around —
and a Phase 3 trial matching it exactly is recruiting, in America, while his family raises
money in Vadodara for a drug India does not manufacture. That is a story about access, not
just a story about a sick child. It is also true.

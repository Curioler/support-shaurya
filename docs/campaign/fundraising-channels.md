## Why the split is costing money, not just tidiness

Crowdfunding runs on visible momentum. A donor deciding whether to give looks at how far
along the campaign is, and two half-filled bars read as weaker than one bar with twice the
progress — even though the money is identical. Splitting also means:

- **No single number to quote.** Every WhatsApp forward, poster and press enquiry has to
  pick a platform, and whichever it picks understates the true total by roughly half.
- **Donors can't tell which is official.** Two links, two family names, two goals. The
  natural reaction to that ambiguity is to wait, not to give.
- **Momentum milestones never trigger.** "We've crossed ₹10 lakh" is a shareable moment
  that has already happened in aggregate — and nobody can announce it.

Whatever else the platform does, **publishing one honest combined total, updated regularly,
with both links underneath, is its single highest-value function.**

## Discrepancies to fix

These are all in live, public copy right now.

### 1. Two different goals — ₹20 crore vs ₹26 crore

ImpactGuru asks for ₹26 crore, Ketto for ₹20 crore. The treating physician's certificate
says **₹26 crore excluding taxes**, so ₹20 crore is below the drug cost alone.

A donor who opens both links sees the family asking for two different amounts for the same
treatment. That is the kind of thing that turns a supporter into a sceptic. Pick one figure
— ideally the full landed cost including duties and hospital charges, per
[open question 29](../open-questions.md) — and use it everywhere.

### 2. The Ketto appeal has the wrong name in it

The Ketto page body reads:

> "My name is **Shaurya Nayak**, and I am fighting to save my son's life."

Shaurya is the four-year-old patient. The campaigner is his mother, Jolly Gaurang Nayak —
which the WhatsApp forward gets right. The page is written in the mother's voice
everywhere else ("my husband is the only earning member"), so this is a single wrong name,
probably from a template. It sits in the second paragraph where every donor will read it.

### 3. ImpactGuru has the relationship inverted

The beneficiary panel reads **"Shaurya Nayak — Parent of Gaurang Nayak."** It is the other
way round. On a page whose selling point is a row of green *Verified* badges, a factually
wrong relationship in the verified section is a bad look. ImpactGuru support can correct it.

### 4. "₹1 lakh spent" appears on both

Still reads oddly against "exhausted our savings", and now it is on two platforms. Ketto's
wording is better — it attributes the spend to "diagnostic tests and lifelong
physiotherapy", which makes ₹1 lakh plausible. ImpactGuru's bare "spent nearly ₹1 lakh on
medical care" next to "exhausted our savings" is the version that invites doubt.

## What the Ketto copy gets right, and should be copied everywhere

The Ketto page is the better-written of the two, and it is medically accurate where it
counts:

- Describes ELEVIDYS as offering "a chance to **slow the progression**" — correct, and
  avoids the "cure" problem in the physician's certificate.
- States the cost as "**excluding taxes and import duties**" — honest about the target
  being a floor.
- Names the treating specialist and hospital: **Dr Ann Agnes Mathew, Sparsh
  Hospital/Virgo Healthcare, Bengaluru**.
- Gives the diagnosis date as **1st May 2026**, which matches the
  [CPK report](../sources/2026-05-01-toprani-labs-cpk-and-blood-group.md) exactly.
- Says the therapy "works best when given at a young age, before irreversible muscle
  damage occurs" — consistent with the physician's certificate.

**One thing it still misses:** the urgency is stated generically. Dr Mathew's certificate
says ELEVIDYS "works in a time limited fashion and works better if given earlier", and that
attributed quote is stronger than any paraphrase.
